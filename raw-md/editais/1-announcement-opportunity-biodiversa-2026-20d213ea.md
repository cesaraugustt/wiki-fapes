## Document 1: Announcement of Opportunity

The Funding Organisations in Biodiversa+ Partnership have joined efforts to organise and fund an international call for transnational research proposals on

## Novel ecosystems: biodiversity, socio-ecological consequences and future trajectories (BiodivFuture)

## 1. Introduction

The Intergovernmental Science-Policy Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services (IPBES) published the Global Assessment Report on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services (IPBES, 2019) which provided unequivocal evidence that biodiversity is declining at a faster rate than at any other time in human history. Multiple anthropogenic direct and indirect drivers are implicated in the unprecedented and rapid change to our natural world. Major direct drivers of biodiversity loss are climate change, land- and sea-use change and intensification, pollution, invasive alien species and over-exploitation of natural resources. Key indirect drivers identified include demographic change, socio-cultural, economic and governance changes, and technological progress. The combined effects of these direct and indirect drivers are leading to detrimental consequences, including negative impacts on ecosystem functioning, water availability and quality, soil quality, food security and nutrition, human, plant and animal health, and resilience to the impacts of climate change (IPBES, 2025). Taken together, our planet and our societies are changing resulting in novel ecosystems (Hobbs et al. 2013; Box 1). Anticipating the future natural world and embedded human social systems create a major challenge for proactively supporting biodiversity and ecosystem services (Evers et al. 2018).

As a response to the global biodiversity crisis, the Convention on Biological Diversity sets out the global 2050 vision of 'Living in harmony with nature', also corresponding to the European Union (EU) vision that, by 2050, biodiversity and its benefits to people will be protected, valued, enhanced and restored (8th EU Environmental Action Programme, EU Biodiversity Strategy for 2030). To achieve these visions, 196 countries adopted in 2022 the Kunming-Montreal Global Biodiversity Framework (KM-GBF, 2022). The framework defines four long-term goals for desirable and resilient socio-ecological systems: (A) to protect, enhance and restore ecosystems; (B) to prosper with nature; (C) to share benefits fairly; and (D) to invest and collaborate.

In this context, the European Biodiversity partnership - Biodiversa+ - is launching a joint research call to support future-looking research and innovation projects that will address biodiversity and novel ecosystems (see Box 1), as well as their socio-ecological dynamics and consequences relating to the sustainability goals of the KM-GBF and associated future

## Box 1. Novel ecosystems - a conceptual framework to set the scene

Novel ecosystems have been defined by Hobbs et al. in 2013 as "a system of abiotic, biotic and social components (and their interactions) that, by virtue of human influence, differ from those that prevailed historically, having a tendency to self-organise, and manifest novel qualities without intensive human management". Since the publication of this paper, the limits of this definition have been challenged by different disciplines and other definitions have emerged. Overall, the conceptual framework of novel ecosystems usually comprises of (1) new ecological communities (including species living outside their historical native ranges), (2) which present a suite of interactions and processes consistent with a self-organised, functioning ecosystem, and (3) are unlikely to revert to conditions preceding their emergence, with or without human intervention, due to significant biotic and abiotic changes. Building on this framework and acknowledging the plurality of views and definitions, proposals submitted to this call must demonstrate how they are addressing novel ecosystems.

pathways and trajectories of change. Anthropogenic influences on nature are leading to far-reaching changes in ecosystems' functions and species compositions, sometimes up to the emergence of novel ecosystems. In fact, only a very limited proportion of ecosystems can still be considered genuinely "natural" or under historically near-pristine conditions. Humandriven changes can have both negative and/or positive outcomes for certain species and ecosystem functions. This raises important questions for biodiversity protection and ecosystem restoration, including how human influence can be adequately considered as a shaping factor, how restoration goals and baselines can be defined under changing conditions, and how ecosystem functions can be maintained or restored in highly modified environment. For example, the implementation of environmental legislation, conservation programmes and sustainable ecosystem management practices in recent decades has, in some contexts, contributed to improvements in the conservation status of species, biodiversity, and certain habitats (EEA, 2020). In any case, the pervasive and accelerating

human influence that defines the Anthropocene will continue to shape and structure novel ecosystems, with a wide range of possible outcomes, that inevitably include risks, opportunities, benefits, and trade-offs. Against this background, this call addresses the need to advance our fundamental understanding of novel ecosystems and their biodiversity, their complex socio-ecological dynamics under current and future trajectories of change, and their ecological, societal, economic and cultural implications, as a basis for a global transition towards ecological sustainability and societal security.

## 2. Objectives of the call

Moving beyond the traditional approach to biodiversity conservation, which focuses on species assemblage with reference to historical baselines, this call focuses on understanding novel ecosystems, their evolution and associated (complex) socio-ecological (incl. biodiversity) dynamics along current and potential future trajectories of change. Applicants should pay particular attention to the ecological functioning as well as the species composition and structure of novel ecosystems, including the present and future environmental, social, economic and political drivers that shape their emergence. They should investigate current trajectories of change and explore future scenarios, encompassing biodiversity, ecosystem functions, and the socio-economic structures and processes that these ecosystems support and are shaped by. Accounting for the diverse world views, applicants should address the risks, opportunities and trade-offs related to novel ecosystems. Intensively managed systems that are highly simplified in their ecological community and processes compared with natural and semi natural ecosystems (e.g. monocultural agricultural systems, intensive aquaculture and plantation forestry) are out of scope. How novel ecosystems interact with and are impacted by these intensively managed systems could be in scope.

<!-- page 3 -->

In the face of inevitable and potentially irreversible change, knowledge generated under this call should ultimately lead to actions to achieve impacts that sustain resilient, interconnected, diverse and ecologically functioning ecosystems. This could include critical reflection on what constitutes ecosystem states that best preserve or restore natural resources by taking multiple costs and benefits (trade-offs) into account and the diversity of contexts, scales, perspectives and world views. Well-functioning ecosystems should benefit people and protect nature at scales from local to global and emphasise biodiversity, trophic complexity, economic vitality, and societal well-being.

Research supported by this call should investigate the causes (drivers), effects (biological responses), and outcomes (including opportunities, risks and trade-offs) of current and future trajectories of change. It should bring to bear the best current understanding of ecosystem assembly rules, of ecosystem dynamics and test our current theories of how ecosystems respond to changes in drivers and composition. It should preferably explore the full range of options to address ongoing change (e.g., the Resist-Accept-Direct [RAD] framework) and identify potential pathways to resilient socio-ecological dynamics that could be supported in a cost-effective manner. The call seeks strategies to better understand and navigate irreversible ecosystem change, including pathways and actions to support resilient outcomes across different ecological and societal contexts. Such outcomes may include the enabling of transitions to new ecological states that are non-degraded, future-resilient ecosystems better adjusted to the new conditions, with halted or reversed biodiversity loss. Alongside this, it can also include approaches aiming to increase societal acceptance of ecological transitions. Additionally, projects should consider how adaptive management of novel ecosystems can ensure and enhance long-term societal acceptance and economic resilience through ongoing monitoring and adjustment of strategies to promote sustainable outcomes, and responsible use of natural resources in the face of uncertainty. It is critical that advances in knowledge consider, respect and account for diverse worldviews and recognise the plurality of nature's values to human societies.

By doing so, projects are expected to support research, knowledge generation and innovation that will contribute to inform policy and interventions to support progress towards the 2050 sustainability goals adopted through the KM-GBF (2022), and to guide future frameworks for action beyond 2050.

## 4. Research priorities

Research priorities to be addressed under this call have been structured into three themes mirroring the KM-GBF sustainability goals (2022). Projects are welcome to address one or several of these themes and may freely combine specific topics. The work plan must be realistic for 3-year projects.

## THEME A - NOVEL ECOSYSTEM FUNCTIONING

Under this theme, projects should focus on understanding the functioning of novel ecosystems and their links to socio-cultural and socio-economic dynamics, identifying mechanisms and developing approaches that can facilitate biodiversity gains. Specific topics that could be considered are (nonexhaustive list):

- ⚫ Understanding novel ecosystem functioning, ecological integrity and trophic interactions, including how functional processes relate to biodiversity outcomes and ecosystem services under future conditions.
- ⚫ Identifying when and how ecological changes become persistent, including changes in species composition, and determining when functional integrity can compensate for compositional change and how it effects ecosystem services.
- ⚫ Assessing how novel ecosystems affect biodiversity, including extinction risk, species persistence and their potential recovery under different environmental and socio-ecological conditions.
- ⚫ Evaluating genetic diversity, adaptive potential and the genetic consequences of rapid species turnover, including implications for resilience under climate change.
- ⚫ Developing models and scenarios to analyse the functioning of novel ecosystems and their ecological and societal implications.
- ⚫ Developing tools (e.g. social, economic, and environmental diagnostics, remote sensing and socioecological decision support systems) to map novel ecosystem conditions and prioritise conservation and restoration interventions, supported by transparent and replicable metrics and inclusive participatory processes.
- ⚫ Evaluating and developing robust quantitative and qualitative indicators compatible with KM-GBF reporting frameworks on the health, quality and functionality of novel ecosystems, while accounting for the socio-ecological dimensions.

## THEME B - PATHWAYS TO NOVEL ECOSYSTEMS

Under this theme, projects should focus on studying robust, plausible, and concrete pathways to novel ecosystems and associated socio-ecological dynamics to prosper under temporal asynchronies and uncertainties. Specific topics that projects could consider under this theme are (non-exhaustive list):

- ⚫ Understanding ecosystem functions, services and disservices in changing socio-ecological systems, including trade-offs and context-specific outcomes across sectors (e.g. urban systems, food, climate)

## 5. Procedures, eligibility and assessment criteria

## 5.1. Submission, deadlines and time schedule

## Submission

A two-Step process will apply, with a mandatory submission of pre-proposals at Step 1 and submission of full proposals at Step 2. Pre-proposals and full proposals (in English) must be submitted electronically with the Electronic Proposal Submission System (EPSS). Instructions for electronic submission will be available on the Biodiversa+ website at https://www.biodiversa.eu/BiodivFuture in September 2026.

Please note that:

- ⚫ The online platform will stay open 5 minutes after the official deadline. Any proposals not correctly submitted at this moment will be declared ineligible.
- ⚫ All completed proposals will be submitted automatically when the platform closes, to avoid a situation where an applicant does not have time to click on the submit button. In this situation, the proposal will be evaluated as it stands.

At Step 1, applicants have to submit pre-proposals: information (in English) on the project consortia, a 5-page description of the project and the required budget for each Partner must be submitted on the EPSS. Submission of pre-proposals is mandatory; it is not possible to enter the procedure at a later stage.

Only eligible pre-proposals can be invited to submit full proposals.

At Step 2, only invited applicants have to submit full proposals: information (in English) on the project consortia, a 16-page description of the project and the required budget for each Partner must be submitted on the EPSS.

The information submitted at Step 1 and Step 2 will be used to complete an eligibility check, to find appropriate evaluators, and to evaluate the pre- (Step 1) and full (Step 2) proposals.

In case very few proposals are received, the CSC can decide with absolute majority to invite all eligible consortia to submit a full proposal in Step 2 without organising an Evaluation Committee in Step 1. Note however that the eligibility check will still be done in Step 1.

## 5.4. Evaluation and Selection

## General information:

Potential applicants are advised to take careful note of the scope and objectives of the call as described above in the Announcement of Opportunity. Applicants are strongly advised to assess the relevance of their proposed research against the scope and objectives of the call set forth in the scientific text of the call; any project that does not fit this will not be recommended for funding, regardless of its quality.

Biodiversa+ aims at funding excellent scientific research projects that can demonstrate societal relevance, including policy relevance, and engage with stakeholders. Proposals will thus be judged on both scientific excellence and their expected impact on society and policy, in addition to project implementation. Proposals should therefore focus on clear research questions with tractable and testable hypotheses and clearly explain expected societal and policy impact as well as their stakeholder engagement approach. Research projects should generate new knowledge and solutions based on the production of new primary data and/or by making use of available data.

Proposals from the natural sciences, technical sciences, and social sciences and humanities are welcome.

The Call Steering Committee (CSC) will establish an EvC, comprising both scientific experts from natural sciences, technical sciences, social sciences and humanities, and policy/management experts relevant to the Call. The EvC composition should allow to cover, as far as possible, the range of topics within the scope of the call.

Members take part in the EvC as independent experts and do not represent any organisation nor can they send any replacements. This means that their work on this Committee does not represent any organisation or nation.

The EvC will assess the proposals according to the criteria defined (see "Assessment criteria" document in the call documents). At Step 2, where relevant (i.e. specifically requested by the EvC), the EvC will also moderate the assessments provided by the external reviewers.

The EvC will discuss the proposals and establish the final ranking of pre- and full proposals based on the set of assessment criteria defined.

After Step 1: The Call Steering Committee (CSC) will decide on which projects to invite to Step 2, following the eligibility check and the evaluation made by the EvC.

After Step 2: The CSC will decide on which projects to recommend for funding, and selection must be made strictly following the final ranking list established by the EvC.

Upon the final decision by the CSC, a list of funded projects will be published on the Biodiversa+ website.

Please note that no appeal can be brought at the EvC and CSC levels to challenge the results of the selection procedure. However, the decisions taken by the CSC do not take precedence over possible mandatory national, regional or organisational requirements for eligibility and appeal processes.

In case of funding failure, an equivalent re-evaluation procedure may be implemented. The exact process will be agreed on by the CSC in an ad hoc manner.

This section sets out the responsibilities of individuals involved in the preparation of research proposals to be submitted under this call.
