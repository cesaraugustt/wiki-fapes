## 1. Introduction

## 1.1. The Joint Transnational Calls in the context of the Water4All partnership

The WatergAll\_partnership started in 2022 under the auspices of the European Union Horizon Europe programme for research and innovation, to concentrate research and innovation on water in Europe and beyond. Water4All's vision is to boost the systemic transformations and changes across the entire research water innovation pipeline, fostering the matchmaking between problem owners and solution providers for ensuring water security for all in the long term"\_

Water security stands for the capacity of a population to safeguard sustainable access to adequate quantities of acceptable quality water for sustaining livelihoods, human well-being, and socio-economic development; water-borne pollution and water-related disasters, and for preserving ecosystems in a climate of peace and political stability. Water security and 'Just' access to clean water for all of society are an essential aspect for Sustainable Development. This is reflected specifically in the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) in first instance SDG 6 "Ensure availability and sustainable management of water and sanitation for all" , which is interconnected with SDGs addressing Poverty (1), (13), Life below Water (14) and on Land (15), Peace, justice and strong institutions (16) and Partnership for the Goals (17).

Reaching Water4All' s objectives require an inclusive and systemic approach, with a logical chain from problem analysis and the identification of knowledge gaps to the uptake of solutions and their concrete applications by the relevant stakeholders. The direct connection between stakeholders and problem owners with solution providers from the RDI field is required to make decisive contributions towards the objectives of Water security: improved access to water; availability of adequate water supplies for all economic uses; ecosystems health and delivery of ecosystem services; resilience to global changes and water-related hazards. This approach will be implemented through a broad range of activities, aiming together at achieving the following outcomes:

- Deliver sound knowledge, tools, and evidence basis on water for policy- &amp; decision-making:
- Improve consideration of water impacts in all relevant policies.
- Enhance the field/market use of innovative solutions to water challenges.
- Increase citizens' awareness and engagement for an inclusive water governance.

Among its activities to produce these outcomes, Water4All will launch a series of annual co-funded Joint Transnational Calls   (JTCs)  pooling national financial resources through the participation of ministries, authorities and funding organisations, together with the support of the European Commission (EC). These calls primarily aim at strengthening the water RDI collaboration and producing and sharing class water-related knowledge and data. The topics for the calls are drawn from the water challenges identified in the Water4All Strategic Research and Innovation Agenda (SRIA). top

The 2026 Joint Transnational Call (JTC) is the sixt in the series of the Water4All co-funded calls. It is jointly launched by 32 Funding Partner Organisations (FPOs) from 30 countries responsible for funding research and innovation actions in the field of water; Out of the participating FPOs, 31 are eligible to the financial support from the EC.

With the aim of developing the capacities of the talented young researchers and giving them the opportunity to act as leaders of the project consortia, Water4All also integrates an independent modality of the Early Career Researchers (ECR) in this JTC. The thematic focus of the ECR modality is aligned with the JTC topics. The applicants can choose whether to apply under the Regular modality or under the modality of ECR, provided they comply with the requirements.

This call also foresees the support to the Knowledge Hub (KH) for the researchers from Transnational Projects funded in the Regular and the ECR modality. The KH will function as a networking platform for the researchers with the aim to synthesize and disseminate the Regular and ECR projects' outcomes in various formats to support evidence-based decision-making processes for targeted stakeholders. The thematic scope of the KH is bounded by the JTC topics.

## 1.2. Objectives of the 2026 Joint Transnational Call

The Water4All Joint Transnational Call 2026 invites research and innovation proposals that address the most pressing challenges for sustainable water management in Europe and beyond, with a particular focus on integrated approaches that strengthen water security, resilience, and the protection of water bodies and dependent ecosystems. The call aims to support projects that improve the understanding, assessment; management, and governance of water resources across scales, including the whole water cycle, from catchment to groundwater and from source to sea, integrating its green water component, while enabling better decision-making for public authorities, water managers, and other stakeholders.

The call seeks to generate new knowledge, tools, methods, and governance approaches that can help tackle competing water demands, improve the efficiency and transparency of water use, and support the transition towards more sustainable and equitable water management systems. Proposals are expected to go beyond the state of the art and to demonstrate clear pathways towards scientific, technical, policy, and societal impact, including uptake by relevant users and alignment with European and international policy frameworks.

In particular, the call will support research and innovation that contributes to improved integrated monitoring, modelling, and assessment of surface water, groundwater, and related socio-environmental systems across the water cycle; better understanding of pressures, risks, and trade-offs related to water quantity and quality , land use, and competing demands from human activities; development and testing of nature-based; hybrid, and data-driven solutions that enhance resilience, recharge, retention, and water availability at catchment and basin scales; more effective and cost-efficient water infrastructures, including monitoring infrastructures, smart systems, and adaptive solutions for ageing networks; and strengthened governance, policy coherence, stakeholder engagement; and social innovation to support inclusive and adaptive water management:. The call also emphasises the role of digital tools, data interoperability, Earth observation; AI, and decision-support systems in enabling integrated water planning and management.

The call is designed to foster multidisciplinary and transdisciplinary collaboration and to encourage proposals that link science, practice, and policy in ways that are relevant across regions and sectors. Projects should consider the broader European context; the need for cross-border cooperation; and the importance of scalable and transferable solutions that can be adapted to different hydrological, institutional, and socio-economic settings.

## 2. Description of the aims and themes of the 2026 Joint Transnational Call

## 2.1. Strategic aim of the Joint Transnational Call

The 2026 Joint Transnational Call within the Water4All Partnership is aiming at research and innovation to contribute to water security in the long term. In line with the strategic objectives of Water4AlI, the results should contribute to the implementation of evidence-based, global, EU and national water management policies and strategies, within the frameworks of the European Green Deal, the zero-pollution action plan, the Water Framework Directive and the two so-called daughter directives on groundwater and quality of surface water, the Nature Restoration Regulation and the EU Biodiversity Strategy for 2030, the Just Transition Mechanism, the Water Resilience Strategy and the Sustainable Development Goals. Proposals should show cognisance of appropriate legislation and policy frameworks that are relevant for the call at national and international levels.

## 2.2. Theme of the Joint Transnational Call

The Water4AlI 2026 Joint Transnational Call focuses on Sustainable Water Management (excluding extreme events and circularity) , inviting proposals that address critical challenges related to Integrated Water Resource Management (IWRM), River Basin Management, Groundwater Management, Water   Resilience, Data monitoring and use, Water-positive solutions.

The call topics are overlapping and non-exclusive. Thus, proposals must address at least one of the following topics and may integrate multiple topics where relevant. It is not compulsory to address all subtopics within a topic in the proposals. Additionally, proposals are encouraged to explore links to other Water4AlI SRIA themes, particularly the SRIA theme "Infrastructures for Water" (excluding extreme events, circularity and health).

In general, collaboration with relevant projects funded under the Horizon Europe programme and the EU Missions should be encourage and overlaps avoided.

## Topic 1: Integrated Monitoring and Assessment for Sustainable Water Management

Reliable, well documented, high-resolution and high-frequency information, available over long time periods, are essential for sustainable integrated water resource management, particularly under increasing pressures from land-use dynamics, competing demands of water, and climate change. This topic addresses the need for integrated monitoring, data (cross)analysis and sharing, and assessment frameworks that enable a holistic understanding of surface and groundwater, and that facilitate robust, evidence-based decision-making. Proposals under this topic should advance innovative monitoring technologies, data integration and/or modelling approaches to enhance our collective understanding of the water cycle and its interconnection with at different spatial and temporal scales as well as decision support tools that facilitate adaptation to changing conditions.

## 1.1 Advanced Monitoring Technologies and Data Interoperability

Contributions should focus on advanced automated monitoring systems, including IoT sensors for real-time and in-situ monitoring of water quality and quantity across the entire water including but not limited to surface and subsurface systems. The use of remote sensing and earth observation technologies to improve the assessment of surface-groundwater interactions, surface-soil interaction, land-use impacts, and climaterelated pressures on water resources is encouraged. Proposals should emphasize data integration and interoperability, as well as improved data accessibility to enable robust assessments, informed decisionmaking, and adaptive management strategies. Particular attention must be to representativeness over time and space, and to the accuracy of data from automated monitoring systems in relation to real environmental levels. cycle paid

## 1.2 Data Integration, Analysis, and Modelling

Proposals should address the integration of GIS-based data to enable a coherent understanding of the spatial and temporal distribution and evolution of surface and groundwater systems. This should involve the combination of heterogeneous cross-sectorial monitoring, observational, and socio-environmental data. To ensure these insights are actionable and policy-oriented, proposals should integrate rigorous water accounting frameworks, such as SEEA-Water 1 to quantify resource availability, abstraction, and consumption in a standardized manner. The focus is placed on big-data analytics, as well as Al and machine learning methods for inter alia anomaly detection, prediction of flows and water levels, calculation of extractable volumes, contaminant dynamics and water quality prevision, and drought and flood risks. The development, coupling, or enhancement of hydrological, hydrogeological, socio-economic models to improve analysis, scenario testing, and forecasting is strongly encouraged. Approaches that enhance model transparency, uncertainty management, data accessibility, and the translation of complex model outputs into intuitive, decision-relevant interfaces are particularly welcome.

## 1.3 Assessment Frameworks, Decision Support, and Socio-Economic Dimensions

Proposals should focus on decision support systems that integrate monitoring data, analytical tools, and/or modelling outcomes in order to facilitate scenario analysis and science-based aid decision-making in situations of uncertainty. There should be a strong emphasis on stakeholder participation, including co-design of decision support systems and co-creation of management pathways with policymakers, water managers, and end users. Research   leading to directly   operational results and addressing   governance structures, institutional coordination; and the cost-efficiency of management measures is particularly encouraged, with the aim of improving the nature-positive effectiveness; socio-economic acceptance, and long-term sustainability of water-related decisions.

## Topic 2: Nature-Based Approaches for Resilient Water Management at   Hydrological and Hydrogeological System Scale

The new hydrological reality and competing land and water uses require integrated and resilient evidencebased approaches to water management at catchment scale from source to sea, based on an enhanced understanding of the entire hydrological This topic focuses on nature-based approaches to enhance groundwater   resilience, improve water  retention and storage, and support adaptive river-basin and groundwater management; as well as enhanced resilience against flood and drought impacts. Proposals should advance scientific understanding, practical implementation; environmental and socio-economic uptake of such measures,  supported by robust  monitoring,   modelling, and stakeholder-oriented frameworks. Water4All is seeking projects that address the positive (co-benefits) and negative impacts both direct and indirect, short-term and long-term of nature-based approaches for water resource management. New scientific knowledge and evidence are sought to assist in the selection and implementation of nature-based solutions that are regionally relevant and beneficial to the environment and society, sustainable over time; and capable of helping to address the challenges of the new hydrological reality. The objective is to strengthen the long-term sustainability and climate resilience of surface and groundwater systems. For that topic; demonstration activities are very welcome. cycle.

## 2.1 Nature-Based Approaches and Managed Aquifer Recharge (MAR) for Sustainable Groundwater Management

Proposals  should assess the effects of nature-based approaches processes, and groundwater quality, including short- and long-term system responses. Strong emphasis should be placed on the design and operation of Managed Aquifer Recharge (MAR) systems under hydrological extremes such as droughts and floods, and their role in enhancing climate resilience. Research on surface - groundwater interactions under climate and land-use change, including thermal influences on groundwater quality, is encouraged. Integrated, process-based, and scalable approaches that support adaptive groundwater management are particularly welcome, especially scalable and transferable solutions.

## 2.2 River-Basin Restoration to Enhance Water Resilience at Catchment Scale

Contributions should focus on the role of retention and restoration measures at catchment scale in improving groundwater recharge, water quality, and long-term water retention through redressing the sponge functions of landscapes. This includes projects that aim to develop sponge targets at landscape and urban levels; such targets quantify how much water soils, wetlands, and ecosystems should retain and how that contributes to increased water resilience by   reducing flood impacts or enhancing   resilience to drought.   Integrated assessments of surface-groundwater interactions and system responses to hydrological   variability and extremes are emphasised. The development and application of digital twins that couple surface water and groundwater processes is strongly encouraged to support simulation; evaluation; and adaptive management of nature-based interventions. Approaches that enhance decision-making, scalability and transferability, and long-term planning for resilient catchment management are particularly welcome.

## 2.3 Economic Valuation and Stakeholder Engagement models for Nature-Based Groundwater and River-Basin Management

Proposals should analyse the economic value of nature-based approaches, including reduced flood and drought impacts, improved groundwater availability, and benefits for agriculture, water supply, pollution reduction, biodiversity and nature, recreation and tourism, and regional economies. Emphasis should be placed on comprehensive benefit assessments addressing the needs of diverse stakeholders such as farmers, water utilities, industry, tourism; and local communities. Research should also explore incentive mechanisms, including co-financing, compensation models, and innovative funding approaches, to support implementation and long-term operation. Frameworks that integrate economic valuation, governance, cross-sector collaboration, and stakeholder engagement to support the increased uptake and scaling of nature-based water management strategies are particularly encouraged.

## Topic 3. Smart, Secure and Adaptive Water Infrastructures for Sustainable Water Management

Increasing pressures on water resources, ageing infrastructure systems, and emerging environmental and geopolitical risks require a transition towards smart; adaptive, and integrated water infrastructures. This topic focuses on enhancing the performance, resilience; and security of water infrastructures across scales, from local systems to river basins.

Proposals under this topic should advance the development and integration of digital, nature-based, and hybrid infrastructure solutions to improve system efficiency, reliability, and flexibility. Emphasis should be placed on data-driven monitoring, adaptive management, and system-level optimisation; as well as on the secure and robust operation of infrastructures under changing conditions. The aim is to support sustainable water management, improve water allocation; and strengthen the long-term resilience of interconnected water systems.

## 3.1. Digital and Risk-informed Water Infrastructures

Proposals should develop and integrate advanced monitoring systems, smart metering, leakage detection, and Al-based analytics to enhance infrastructure performance and decision-making: There should be a strong emphasis on interoperable data platforms, digital twins, and real-time data integration to support system optimisation. Research should also address risk and hazard assessment, early warning systems, and operational security, including cyber and physical threats. Data-driven approaches that improve reliability, transparency, and adaptive capacity are particularly encouraged.

## 3.2. Modernisation and Adaptive Asset Management of Ageing Infrastructures

Proposals should focus on innovative approaches for upgrading, retrofitting, and extending the lifetime of existing water infrastructures. There should be an emphasis on modular, flexible, and cost-efficient solutions that can respond to evolving demands, climate variability, and emerging risks. Research is encouraged on the integration of smart technologies into legacy systems, as well as on improving safety, robustness, and operational continuity. Approaches supporting long-term planning, prioritisation of investments, and adaptive asset management are particularly welcome.

## 3.3. Integrated and Hybrid Infrastructures for Basin-scale Management

Proposals should investigate solutions that enhance coordination between built infrastructures and natural water systems, including interactions between surface water and groundwater systems. There should be a strong emphasis on hybrid grey-green and centralised-decentralised systems that support integrated water resources management. Research should also address infrastructures enabling efficient, transparent, and equitable water allocation across sectors, water grids and users, while avoiding unintended effects such as rebound increases in water use. System-level, scalable, and governance-aware approaches are particularly encouraged.

## Topic 4. Resilient Water Systems Through Systemic Change, Policy Coherence and Inclusive Governance

This topic aims to strengthen the resilience of river basins, groundwater systems, and coastal waters by integrating systemic approaches with coherent policies and inclusive governance and improved understanding of water's strategic value. It emphasizes integrated perspectives that recognise the physical, ecological, and socio-economic connections across the entire water cycle. The proposals under this topic are expected to develop harmonized knowledge, tools and models that address emerging environmental, societal, and geopolitical challenges, and improve cross-sectoral and transboundary cooperation capacities. The proposals are also expected to underline regulatory challenges and policy coherence across sectors, as well as relevant European frameworks and strategies (e.g. the Water Framework Directive, Urban Wastewater Treatment Directive, Water Reuse Regulation; Water Resilience Strategy). Proposals should consider how governance and policy instruments can support integrated, sustainable, and adaptive water management across scales and sectors.

## 4.1 Systemic Approaches for Social, Economic and Environmental Resilience

Increasing disruptions in the hydrological cycle are creating unprecedented pressures on resilience of incremental change. There is a need to move beyond narrow problem definitions and instead explore how water both influences and is influenced by interactions across ecosystems and human activities. Examination of cascading effects, feedback loops, and trade-offs across spatial, temporal; and societal scales and sectors would help understand system interactions.

In this subtopic; transdisciplinary research teams should contribute to increased understanding of system boundary definitions, integration of diverse environmental stressors and consequences, and effective ways of stakeholder involvement for systemic solutions. The overarching aim is to generate holistic knowledge and tools that support sound decision-making and sustainable water management practices for systemic change towards resilient water systems that can respond to future environmental and societal challenges.

## 4.2 Policy Coherence and Inclusive Governance for AlI

Fragmented and incoherent governance frameworks constrain effective decision-making, investment, and coordinated action across sectors, scales, and borders.

This subtopic focuses on advancing integrated and inclusive approaches to water governance that connect ecosystem processes, societal participation, and economic valuation and innovative finance models in support of   resilient water systems. Proposals   should address conjunctive management of  surface water and groundwater, as well as interactions across the water cycle, while strengthening the links between governance, infrastructure planning; water allocation, sustainable water use and associated economic models. Particular attention should be given to improving policy coherence across sectors such as water, agriculture, energy, and land use, and to aligning governance and financial frameworks with long-term climate and sustainability objectives.

## 2.3. Cross-cutting themes/aspects/considerations

Proposals should also consider the following cross-cutting aspects where relevant:

- Climate change and environmental pressures: Evaluating how climate variability and change, including hydrological extremes such as droughts and floods, affect water availability, water quality, and ecosystem functioning: Proposals should consider implications for long-term resilience and adaptive water management strategies.
- Socio-economic and participatory aspects: Investigating stakeholder engagement, public perceptions, behavioural   change, and water use practices, as well as the socio-economic implications of water management decisions. Approaches that support equitable, inclusive, and participatory water governance are encouraged.
- Digitalisation and data-driven approaches: Promoting the development and integration of digital tools, monitoring systems, and data-driven methods,   including artificial   intelligence; to support water management, system optimisation; and decision-making across scales.
- Innovation uptake and implementation: Strategies to facilitate the adoption; scaling, and real-world implementation of innovative solutions, including technological, nature-based, and governance approaches; while addressing barriers to uptake and ensuring long-term sustainability and impact.

## 2.4. Expected approach

The proposals presented to the Water4All 2026 JTC are expected to take a comprehensive and problemoriented approach. They should demonstrate how the proposed research addresses the overarching goals of the call and contribute to improving sustainable water management:. Likewise, the proposals must avoid too specific and narrowly defined cases; thus, they are expected to address aspects that are cross-cutting and highlight cases where the transfer of solutions to other contexts and regions is feasible and can have a higher impact.

Delivered knowledge needs to cover relevant existing gaps and have the clear potential to be used in new or improved tools, processes, policy options and policy making. The proposals are also expected to contribute to the acceleration of the adoption of measures and to connect actors. In all cases, knowledge transfer and the impact of the outputs must be credible through involvement of stakeholders.

Given the complexity of water challenges and their cross-sectorial impacts, proposals may need to take a multi- or interdisciplinary approach; not as an end in itself, but to better define problems, develop solutions, and deliver them effectively. Proposals must not duplicate, but can complement, past efforts and results delivered by projects and actions previously funded by e.g. Horizon 2020, Horizon Europe, Water JPI, Water4AlI, Biodiversa+ , PRIMA and JPI AMR calls. Approaches built on the results of previous projects of the mentioned instruments, such as in e.g. BiodivNBS Call 2023-2024, must demonstrate that they include substantially new or improved ideas and approaches for additional progress and impact.

## 2.5. Impact of the proposals

Proposals are expected to demonstrate clear and credible impacts extending beyond the duration of the project, contributing to more sustainable, resilient, and equitable water management systems. They should identify the main beneficiaries of the proposed work and explain how the expected results are transferable to other  locations and scales, and development; and societal uptake.

The call aims to deliver impacts at several levels, including improved understanding of water systems, stronger decision-support capacity, improved resilience of water systems and water dependent sectors, increased and long-term water availability, and more effective responses to current and emerging water management challenges.

In particular, projects should contribute to improved assessment and forecasting of water quantity and quality, groundwater dynamics, and related risks across the water cycle; increased adoption of integrated water management approaches that account for multiple pressures, cross-sector interactions, and spatial and temporal scales; development and implementation of nature-based solutions, including their integration with technological and hybrid approaches; enhanced affordability, effectiveness and adaptability of both existing and future water   infrastructures; and strengthened governance and policy frameworks that support coordination across sectors and administrative levels in water allocation and management.

Proposals should also describe how their outcomes support relevant European and international policy frameworks, including water-related environmental, resilience, and sustainability objectives. This includes demonstrating how project results can contribute to the implementation of European strategies (e.g. relevant directives and frameworks) , and to improved integrated water management, enhanced protection of water may

Societal and economic impacts are also expected to be clearly addressed. Projects should explain how their results may improve water security, support more efficient use of public and private resources, reduce risks and costs associated with water stress or poor water management; and generate solutions that are transferable to other regions and contexts.

Credible impact will depend on active collaboration with stakeholders throughout the project lifecycle, from problem definition to dissemination and uptake of results. Proposals should therefore include appropriate engagement with end users, policy actors, water managers, industry, civil society, and other relevant groups to facilitate knowledge transfer, implementation; and long-term adoption of the project outcomes.

## 5. Evaluation of Proposals

## 5.1. Evaluation Procedures

two-step evaluation procedure will apply, for the pre-proposals (step 1) and the full proposal (step 2) respectively. The evaluation procedure will be common for all proposals submitted under the JTC and ECR modality and regardless the topics.

The CSC will set up an Evaluation Panel (EP), which will be comprised of international scientific experts from the relevant research areas covered in this call. Its composition will consider the gender and geographical balance. The EP members cannot be applicants in the joint call and cannot evaluate proposals with which they declared a conflict of interest; to ensure a fair evaluation process and equal treatment of applicants.

The CS will check that no Conflict of Interest exists concerning the proposals evaluated.

The EP will be headed by a Chair and a Vice-Chair. The Chair and Vice-Chair will be in charge of moderating the EP meetings and conveying the results of the discussions to the CSC.

All proposals, either submitted under the JTC or the ECR modality will be evaluated by the same EP and in accordance with the same evaluation criteria (see section 5.2) and ranked in one or several ranking list(s) per modality and/or per topics.

An independent observer will be invited as external observer to the EP and CSC meetings to assess the conformity of the general procedure with the EU regulation.

## 5.6. Widening Option

The "widening" option between the pre-proposal stage and the full-proposal stage aims to maximize the number of countries/regions involved into the projects funded.

Consortia invited to submit a full proposal in step 2 of the evaluation process will have the possibility to include in their consortium; either on request of the Funding Partner Organisations (FPOs) or on a voluntary basis, a new partner established in an underrepresented country and FPO between Step 1 and Step 2. The list of underrepresented countries and FPOs will be published on Water4All website after Step 1 outcomes.

The addition of a new partner should not substantially change the proposals' content but should be relevant and enhance the original focus of the proposal.

The addition of a partner from an underrepresented funding organisation does not count towards the maximum changes allowed between step 1 and 2 (cf. section 6.2). However, eligibility procedure remains the same and national/regional eligibility rules still apply. Consortia are invited to check the Water4All Partnering Tool for potential suitable partners.

Please note that all changes in the consortium composition should be conducted in close collaboration with the concerned Funding Partner Organisation and requests for changes shall be assessed and allowed by all the FPOs involved in the proposal.

The optimisation of the impact of publicly-funded scientific research is of fundamental importance to improve conditions to i) minimise the time spent searching for information and accessing it, ii) be able to speed up scientific progress, and iii) make it easier to cooperate across and beyond the EU.

Open form publications produced by the Transnational Projects funded by the 2026 JTC will support Green Open Access (immediate or delayed open access provided through self-archiving), or Gold Open Access (immediate open access provided by a publisher for the most important outputs) . In the case of Green Open Access, partners will (as soon as possible and at the latest on publication) deposit a machine-readable electronic copy of the published version or final peerreviewed manuscript accepted for publication in a repository for scientific publications. In addition; partners will ensure open access to the deposited publication and bibliographic metadata. Information should be provided about all the other scholarly objects, tools and instruments that are needed to validate the conclusions of the publication. long

In case of any Intellectual Property Rights (IPR) issue for some project data and products the consortium may decide to go for Opt Out Option and cover this within the project consortium agreement. Projects can opt out before or after the signature of the Consortium Agreement (thereby freeing themselves from the associated obligations) on the following grounds:

- Incompatibility with the need for confidentiality in connection with security issues;
- Incompatibility with rules on protecting personal data;
- If there are other legitimate reasons not to provide open access to research data.

Depending on the funding rules of the FPOs, the costs related to the implementation of these provisions may be eligible for reimbursement during the grant.

The funded Transnational Projects are requested to develop a Data Management Plan within the first six months of the project's lifetime, according to the provisions of the HE MGA art. 17, page 110 1126, and are expected to appoint FAIR contact points.

The funded projects will be requested to submit metadata on all the resources directly generated by the project, as well as additional information on how this data will be used, if and how the data will be made accessible for verification and re-use, and how the data will be curated and preserved.
